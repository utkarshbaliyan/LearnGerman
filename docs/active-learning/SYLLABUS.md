# Active Learning — complete module syllabus

Original curriculum proposal • 13 September 2026 • 12 modules per level, four paired lessons per module.

Each numbered lesson below supplies **one speaking activity and one writing activity**, normally on separate visits. Input, support and task length follow `COURSE-DESIGN.md`. The communication goal leads; the language column identifies content to teach or refresh before production. Every module has a later, unfamiliar speaking and writing check. Teacher-authored scripts, input recordings and calibrated scoring exemplars remain to be produced before release.

All learners cover personal, public, educational and professional life. School/college and office prompts offer relevant role choices. Fictional identities and family details are always allowed. Health, banking and administrative tasks practise communication, not professional advice.

## Language sequencing guardrails

A1 prioritises useful personal phrases, present tense, basic questions, negation, articles in familiar chunks, numbers/time, possession, requests and simple location. It does not need every case table or a perfect-tense narrative on day one. A2 explicitly bridges any missing high-frequency past forms, then develops descriptions, comparisons, routine reasons, subordinate-clause chunks and practical object/preposition use. B1 consolidates flexible tense use, connected reasons, references, relative clauses, polite hypothetical requests and everyday text organisation. Advanced reported speech, elaborate nominalisation and perfect control of every exception are not mandatory B1 communication gates.

These are teaching choices, not an official CEFR grammar inventory. Every released task needs a prerequisite review and language sample review. All four tasks in a module share a theme but must vary meaningful content, audience or purpose; simply increasing word count is not progression.
