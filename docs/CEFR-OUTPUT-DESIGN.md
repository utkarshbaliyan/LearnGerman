# Chapter output progression

The previous speaking bank repeated ten missions irrespective of chapter or level. It has been replaced with 72 paired writing/speaking tasks keyed to the 72 existing grammar lessons. Each task has an explicit output size, question sequence and server-owned feedback expectations.

- A1 chapters 1–3: two short sentences; two short spoken answers. Chapter 1 uses personal pronouns and sein, not appointment negotiation, explanations or long monologues.
- A1 chapters 4–12: three short sentences and two questions. Chapters 13–24: four to five short sentences and three questions. Past-tense output starts at chapter 24, where it is taught.
- A2 chapters 1–12: four to six sentences and three questions. Chapters 13–24: six to eight sentences and four questions. Rescheduling with a reason is at chapter 16, alongside weil/da/dass.
- B1 chapters 1–12: a short connected text around 80–100 words and four questions. Chapters 13–24: around 100–140 words and five questions. These are design guidelines, not CEFR-mandated word counts or grading cutoffs. Reported speech does not require Konjunktiv I.

A1 partner questions are authored and deterministic to prevent topic escalation. A2/B1 replies must follow the chapter's planned questions and limits. AI feedback is instructed to accept the level's expected limitations and focus on one A1 error or at most two A2/B1 errors. Short A1 answers such as Ja are accepted.

Empty profiles are hidden. Extra practice, photo input, alternate speaking modes, privacy controls and deeper grammar reference material use progressive disclosure. Consent and transcript confirmation remain visible. Completion uses the existing objective practice checks, not obsolete AI speaking/writing scores; practice completion does not establish language mastery. Existing session history is retained when switching from the previous speaking task; incompatible active missions require a fresh start.

Basis: Council of Europe [CEFR descriptors](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search), [self-assessment grid](https://rm.coe.int/16802fc1bf), and [Goethe level descriptions](https://www.goethe.de/en/m/spr/kur/stu.html). A1 allows simple formulaic phrases with support; A2 focuses on routine familiar exchanges; B1 develops connected accounts and reasons.

This is a redesign of chapter output tasks, feedback expectations and course presentation. Existing story texts and grammar exercise banks have not undergone a full teacher-led CEFR calibration. CEFR does not prescribe an exact grammar syllabus or certify these chapter assignments. Teacher review and a beginner pilot remain necessary before claiming validated alignment or learning gains.

## Simplified speaking drill

The student now sees one chapter question immediately, records an answer, and stops to receive feedback and corrections automatically. Retry stays on that question; Next question is optional. There is no mission setup, mode selection or mandatory transcript-confirmation step. Learners may correct speech-recognition mistakes and request updated feedback. Unconfirmed transcripts are excluded from shared error/independent-use evidence. Corrections are shown immediately for speaking, so later retries are recorded as correction-assisted. The writing repair workflow is unchanged. Existing mission history is retained separately from new drill history.
