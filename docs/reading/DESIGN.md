# Reading path, edition 1

The default Stories route is an original 72-story reading strand: 24 stories each at A1, A2 and B1. All 72 course chapters use the same corresponding story text, useful phrases and meaning questions. The previous 440-story library remains at `/stories/previous`; it is not presented as the new graded path.

## Progression

Four sections of six stories per level. The opening A1 scene is 26 words. The first section uses very short exchanges, concrete objects and familiar present-tense language. A1 grows into practical messages, invitations, directions and a supported first account in the perfect tense. A2 grows through shared tasks, choices, past experiences, explanations and practical plans. B1 uses connected accounts, competing priorities, requests, reports and communication across different viewpoints.

Length is an editorial tool, not a CEFR requirement or a guarantee of difficulty. Avoid padding. Returning characters and objects support continuity; different events and questions provide new contexts. More advanced structures already in the grammar course (notably past Konjunktiv II and recognition of Konjunktiv I) are explicitly labelled supported extensions, not assumed entry-level B1 knowledge.

The visible path offers one next story, level-only tabs and four collapsible sections. The reader opens on the German text. Word meanings, an English gist, useful expressions with exact story examples, and a grammar link are available without showing a large tutorial first. Two meaning questions allow retries. Finishing them means practice completed, not certified proficiency or independent transfer.

## Vocabulary

Each story has 3–5 focus expressions, a form actually present in the text and an exact paragraph as context. Inflected/separated verb forms are deliberately distinguished from dictionary headwords. Incidental vocabulary remains available through word tooltips. The new reading gloss supplement supplies missing meanings without the old assumption that a capitalised word must be a name. A deterministic exposure map links repeated focus vocabulary to earlier stories; it does not claim that exposure equals acquisition.

The course uses canonical dictionary headwords for vocabulary review. The 112 newly connected dictionary cards bring the shared catalog to 4,123 entries; three fixed expressions stay as contextual reader help. Existing card IDs and learned states are preserved.

`coverage.csv` records every story's topic, grammar focus, chapter, word count and target vocabulary. `topic-coverage.json` maps broad publisher topic domains to original scenes. Neither is a word-for-word coverage claim for a commercial textbook. The existing vocabulary course continues to supply wider deliberate practice; these 72 stories alone do not teach all 4,000 vocabulary entries.

## Course integration and saved work

Course grammar, writing/speaking tasks and existing learned-word state are preserved. The new course reading text replaces the previous randomly spaced legacy story. Its listening question and reading question address different details. A six-item checkpoint combines grammar review and the new story. Focus vocabulary is deliberately small; incidental words are learned in context rather than assigned as 30 mandatory new cards on day one.

Completion uses new versioned story IDs. Course comprehension and checkpoint evidence is stored per story edition and merged by latest timestamp, including lower scores. Earlier course and story records remain stored; they cannot automatically certify the new edition. Grammar progress is reused. Story completion is shared between the library and course after both meaning questions have been checked correctly; overall chapter completion still requires its separate checks.

Current narration uses the browser's German speech synthesis of the current text, with slow/normal speed, cancellation and error handling. It never plays an old recording over new text. Voice availability and quality depend on the device. Professionally reviewed and consistently recorded narration remains a quality improvement, not an implemented claim.

## Reference scope and remaining validation

Use publisher contents to check breadth, never to copy stories, exercises or characters. The stories, plots, questions and English support here are original. CEFR is a framework of communicative abilities; it does not prescribe one exhaustive German grammar list or word count per story.
