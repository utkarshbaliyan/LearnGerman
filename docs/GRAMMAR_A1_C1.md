# German grammar: core A1–C1 curriculum

Research date: 6 October 2026.

144 released lessons: 24 each at A1, A2 and B1; 36 each at B2 and C1. Every lesson has 50 tasks in five sets of ten. The expansion adds 72 lessons, 720 distinct original model contexts, 720 sentence-specific grammatical explanations and 3,600 practice tasks, bringing the total to 7,200. Recognition, fill, order, correction, both translation directions and guided production retain the existing interaction model.

## Scope and limits

This is comprehensive core standard German grammar through C1, with an editorial level sequence. CEFR describes communicative competence and does not provide an exhaustive German grammar checklist. Lexical valency, regional forms, idiom and specialist linguistic analysis remain open-ended. Sources inform the rules and communicative goals; they do not certify the lessons. Content has not been independently teacher reviewed or CEFR certified.

Explanations and examples are original paraphrases and original sentences. Primary references are IDS grammis, the official spelling rules, Council of Europe CEFR descriptors and Goethe model examination materials.

## Practice and progress

Advanced fixed-answer exercises request the taught form and neutral model wording. Translation word banks constrain wording; alternative translations may be valid. Advanced grading checks capitals. Existing A1–B1 grading semantics remain case-insensitive; punctuation spacing is normalised and sentence-building now correctly joins commas, colons and semicolons. Guided production is explicitly self-checked; its legacy completion value remains unchanged for progress compatibility and is no longer displayed as independently assessed mastery.

All prior lesson IDs, exercise IDs, practice-group names, storage keys and best-score merge semantics remain intact. No database migration, authentication change, quota change or learner-data rewrite is required. Corrections to old explanations cover 2024 infinitive comma rules, mixed noun declension, genitive variation and reporting attribution. A few valid alternatives formerly shown as errors were replaced.

## Topic coverage

### A1 1: Sentence foundations

- a1-1-1: Personal pronouns and sein — Introduce people and say who or what they are.
- a1-1-2: Regular verbs in the present — Conjugate common verbs and describe everyday actions.
- a1-1-3: Irregular present-tense verbs — Use frequent stem-changing verbs confidently.
- a1-1-4: Main-clause word order — Keep the conjugated verb in the correct position.
- a1-1-5: Questions — Ask natural yes/no and W-questions.
- a1-1-6: Negation with nicht and kein — Choose the right negative form and place it correctly.

### A1 2: Nouns and the first cases

- a1-2-1: Gender and definite articles — Learn nouns together with der, die, or das.
- a1-2-2: Indefinite articles — Use ein and eine when something is not yet specific.
- a1-2-3: Plural forms — Recognize and form the most useful noun plurals.
- a1-2-4: The nominative — Identify the subject of a sentence.
- a1-2-5: The accusative — Mark direct objects with the correct article.
- a1-2-6: Possessive determiners — Express my, your, his, her, and our.

### A1 3: Everyday verb patterns

- a1-3-1: Modal verbs — Use können, müssen, wollen, dürfen, and sollen.
- a1-3-2: Separable verbs — Place prefixes correctly in statements and questions.
- a1-3-3: The imperative — Give friendly and formal instructions.
- a1-3-4: mögen and möchten — Express preferences and polite wishes.
- a1-3-5: es gibt and haben — Say what exists and what someone has.
- a1-3-6: Reflexive verbs: first steps — Use common daily-routine verbs with mich and dich.

### A1 4: Time, place, and connection

- a1-4-1: The dative: first steps — Recognize common indirect objects and dative articles.
- a1-4-2: Common dative prepositions — Use mit, nach, bei, seit, von, and zu.
- a1-4-3: Two-way prepositions: location — Describe where something is with the dative.
- a1-4-4: Time expressions — Say when, how often, and for how long.
- a1-4-5: Coordinating conjunctions — Connect clauses with und, aber, oder, and denn.
- a1-4-6: The perfect tense: introduction — Report completed everyday actions with haben and sein.

### A2 1: Cases and prepositions

- a2-1-1: The dative system — Use dative articles and pronouns with confidence.
- a2-1-2: Two-way prepositions: movement — Contrast location and direction.
- a2-1-3: Accusative prepositions — Use durch, für, gegen, ohne, and um.
- a2-1-4: Dative prepositions — Use aus, außer, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, and zu.
- a2-1-5: Possession: von and genitive — Express ownership in spoken and written German.
- a2-1-6: Adjective endings after definite articles — Describe known nouns accurately.

### A2 2: Description and reference

- a2-2-1: Adjective endings with ein-words — Describe nouns after ein, kein, and possessives.
- a2-2-2: Comparison — Use comparative, superlative, als, and wie.
- a2-2-3: Demonstratives and welcher — Point to and select specific people or things.
- a2-2-4: Relative clauses: nominative — Add information about a subject.
- a2-2-5: Relative clauses: accusative — Add information about an object.
- a2-2-6: Reflexive and reciprocal meaning — Distinguish sich from einander.

### A2 3: Past, future, and subordinate clauses

- a2-3-1: The perfect tense — Choose haben or sein and build participles.
- a2-3-2: sein, haben, and modals in the preterite — Use the most frequent spoken past forms.
- a2-3-3: Future meaning — Express plans with the present and predictions with werden.
- a2-3-4: weil, da, and dass — Move the conjugated verb to the end.
- a2-3-5: wenn and als — Separate repeated conditions from one-time past events.
- a2-3-6: Indirect questions — Form polite questions with ob and W-words.

### A2 4: Purpose, sequence, and voice

- a2-4-1: Infinitives with zu — Connect two verb ideas economically.
- a2-4-2: um … zu and damit — Express purpose with the same or different subject.
- a2-4-3: Dative and accusative objects — Order two objects naturally.
- a2-4-4: Time–manner–place — Organize information in the middle field.
- a2-4-5: deshalb, trotzdem, and sonst — Connect cause, contrast, and consequence.
- a2-4-6: The present passive — Focus on processes instead of the actor.

### B1 1: Precision with nouns and pronouns

- b1-1-1: Adjective endings: complete system — Choose endings across articles and cases.
- b1-1-2: The genitive — Express possession and use common genitive prepositions.
- b1-1-3: Advanced relative clauses — Use dative, genitive, and prepositional relatives.
- b1-1-4: N-declension — Decline frequent masculine nouns correctly.
- b1-1-5: da- and wo-compounds — Refer naturally to things and ideas with prepositions.
- b1-1-6: Indefinite pronouns — Use man, jemand, niemand, etwas, and nichts.

### B1 2: Narrative time, voice, and modality

- b1-2-1: The narrative preterite — Use common written past forms in connected narration.
- b1-2-2: The pluperfect — Show what happened before another past event.
- b1-2-3: Future and assumptions — Use werden for future meaning and probability.
- b1-2-4: Passive in present and past — Describe processes across time.
- b1-2-5: Passive alternatives — Use man, lassen, and sich lassen naturally.
- b1-2-6: Konjunktiv II: present — Make polite, hypothetical, and unreal statements.

### B1 3: Complex clause architecture

- b1-3-1: Konjunktiv II: past — Discuss unreal past situations and regrets.
- b1-3-2: Conditions with wenn and falls — State real and hypothetical conditions.
- b1-3-3: Contrast clauses — Use obwohl, während, and wohingegen.
- b1-3-4: Time clauses — Use bevor, nachdem, seitdem, bis, and sobald.
- b1-3-5: Infinitive clauses and reductions — Avoid repetition with compact structures.
- b1-3-6: Paired conjunctions — Use sowohl … als auch, weder … noch, and related pairs.

### B1 4: Connected and appropriate German

- b1-4-1: Nominalisation — Turn verbs and adjectives into useful nouns.
- b1-4-2: Participles as adjectives — Compress information inside noun phrases.
- b1-4-3: Reported speech — Report statements with dass and common Konjunktiv I forms.
- b1-4-4: Object and pronoun order — Place pronouns, nouns, and negation naturally.
- b1-4-5: Cohesion and reference — Link paragraphs with pronouns and connective adverbs.
- b1-4-6: Commas and formal register — Punctuate complex clauses and adjust style to context.

### B2 1: Complements and verb chains

- b2-1-1: Verb valency and case frames — Choose objects from the verb’s lexical frame, rather than translating English word order.
- b2-1-2: Prepositional verbs and correlates — Use fixed prepositions, case and da-compounds before clauses.
- b2-1-3: Adjective and noun complements — Complete adjective and noun phrases with the correct case or preposition.
- b2-1-4: Who performs the infinitive? — Identify subject control, object control and when a dass-clause is needed.
- b2-1-5: Bare infinitives: lassen, perception and brauchen — Distinguish bare infinitives from zu-infinitives in frequent verb combinations.
- b2-1-6: Ersatzinfinitiv and auxiliary position — Build perfect modal chains and place haben before a double infinitive in subordinate clauses.

### B2 2: Time and passive perspective

- b2-2-1: Tense, reference time and narrative sequence — Select tense from the intended time relationship rather than an English tense label.
- b2-2-2: Futur II: completion and assumptions — Express completion by a future deadline and an inference about a past event.
- b2-2-3: Perfect and passive infinitives — Relate an infinitive action to an earlier time and distinguish active from passive.
- b2-2-4: Process passive and state passive — Distinguish an action being carried out from the resulting state.
- b2-2-5: Modal passive across tenses — Express duties, possibilities and past requirements with a passive verb chain.
- b2-2-6: Impersonal and recipient passive — Describe activities without an accusative patient and foreground a recipient.

### B2 3: Reporting and degrees of certainty

- b2-3-1: Konjunktiv I and replacement forms — Attribute information using the full reported-speech system without implying automatic disbelief.
- b2-3-2: Reported time, pronouns and place — Report past and future statements while preserving who spoke, where and when.
- b2-3-3: Reported questions and requests — Embed yes/no questions, W-questions, instructions and requests in a report.
- b2-3-4: Epistemic modals: present probability — Distinguish obligation and ability from inference about what is true.
- b2-3-5: Epistemic modals about the past — Infer what happened using a modal and a perfect infinitive.
- b2-3-6: Hearsay and claims: sollen and wollen — Attribute unverified information and distinguish a person’s claim from their intention.

### B2 4: Conditions and logical relationships

- b2-4-1: Mixed-time counterfactual conditions — Connect an unreal past condition to a present result without confusing their time frames.
- b2-4-2: Conditions without wenn and unreal wishes — Form verb-first conditions and independent wishes with clear counterfactual meaning.
- b2-4-3: Unreal comparison: als ob and als — Describe an appearance or comparison without asserting that it is factual.
- b2-4-4: Means, absence and replacement — Connect actions with indem, dadurch dass, ohne dass/zu and statt dass/zu.
- b2-4-5: Consequences and degree clauses — Distinguish a result from purpose and connect degree with a possible or impossible outcome.
- b2-4-6: Proportional comparison: je … desto — Connect changing quantities and preserve the different clause orders.

### B2 5: Dense noun phrases and reference

- b2-5-1: Extended participial attributes — Compress a relative clause into an attributive phrase while retaining case and agreement.
- b2-5-2: Gerundive attributes: zu + Partizip I — Describe a required or possible action on a noun using a formal participial attribute.
- b2-5-3: Nominal and verbal style — Convert clauses to noun phrases while preserving actor, object and logical relation.
- b2-5-4: Free relatives and sentence reference — Refer to unspecified people, things and whole propositions with appropriate relative structures.
- b2-5-5: The different jobs of es — Distinguish referential es, impersonal subjects, correlates and first-field placeholders.
- b2-5-6: Word formation and grammatical categories — Interpret compound heads, derived nouns and separable or inseparable prefixes.

### B2 6: Discourse, spelling and synthesis

- b2-6-1: Sentence fields and the Nachfeld — Place clauses and long phrases around the verb bracket without losing the finite verb.
- b2-6-2: Neutral order and focused order — Use pronouns and information structure without mistaking flexible word order for a universal error.
- b2-6-3: Negation and scope — Choose nicht or kein and identify which claim or constituent is being rejected.
- b2-6-4: Focus particles and modal particles — Interpret only, even, expectation and conversational attitude without treating particles as filler.
- b2-6-5: Current punctuation and spelling essentials — Apply current comma rules, recognise integrated infinitives and avoid common spelling traps.
- b2-6-6: B2 synthesis: revise a project update — Combine grammar choices in a practical update and justify revisions instead of memorising isolated forms.

### C1 1: Lexical precision and complex time

- c1-1-1: Rare case frames and mixed declension — Handle genitive complements, double accusatives and nouns that differ from the regular weak pattern.
- c1-1-2: Valency changes with meaning — Choose a construction from the intended sense of a verb and preserve it when paraphrasing.
- c1-1-3: Integrated and separate infinitive constructions — Distinguish a unified predicate from a separate infinitive clause and interpret control carefully.
- c1-1-4: Long modal, perfect and passive chains — Assemble demanding verb groups by separating tense, voice and modal meaning.
- c1-1-5: Relative time in nested clauses — Keep before, during and after relationships clear across several clause levels.
- c1-1-6: Future in the past and reported perspective — Express later events viewed from a past point without importing automatic English tense shifts.

### C1 2: Voice, modality and careful inference

- c1-2-1: Passive limits and case preservation — Recognise where passive is possible and avoid mechanical subject–object swapping.
- c1-2-2: Recipient perspective and register — Use bekommen and erhalten constructions deliberately and contrast them with active and werden-passive.
- c1-2-3: Precise passive alternatives and modality — Preserve obligation, possibility and agency when transforming passive sentences.
- c1-2-4: Synthetic Konjunktiv II and würde choices — Choose clear potential or counterfactual forms while avoiding unnecessary würde chains.
- c1-2-5: Counterfactual concession and excluded consequence — Use unreal concession, irrealis consequences and modal counterfactuals with distinct time reference.
- c1-2-6: Evidence, certainty and hedging — Distinguish strong inference, possibility, cautious probability and attributed evidence.

### C1 3: Attribution and refined clause relations

- c1-3-1: Sustained indirect reporting — Maintain attribution, person and tense across a paragraph rather than one isolated reported sentence.
- c1-3-2: Attribution versus assertion — Make the boundary between source claims, observed facts and your own conclusions explicit.
- c1-3-3: Restricted and exceptional conditions — Express conditions, limits and exceptions with sofern, soweit, vorausgesetzt and es sei denn.
- c1-3-4: Concession and contrast in formal prose — Distinguish conceded facts, hypothetical concessions and direct contrasts.
- c1-3-5: Formal cause, grounds and consequences — Use causal and consequential connectors with their correct syntactic class.
- c1-3-6: Restriction, explanation and alternatives — Limit a statement, explain a term and formulate alternative relationships without connector confusion.

### C1 4: Noun phrase precision and cohesion

- c1-4-1: Layered attributes and ambiguity — Read and compose complex noun phrases while controlling attachment and scope.
- c1-4-2: Apposition and case agreement — Use close titles, explanatory appositions and comparison phrases without losing case.
- c1-4-3: Agreement with quantities and coordinated subjects — Identify the grammatical head and recognise genuine agreement variation with quantities.
- c1-4-4: Nominalisation with explicit participants — Avoid ambiguous genitives and preserve roles, time and modality in formal noun phrases.
- c1-4-5: Derivation, prefix stress and lexical limits — Connect word formation with separability, participles and changes of meaning.
- c1-4-6: Reference chains and recoverable ellipsis — Keep people and propositions identifiable and omit only material that readers can reconstruct.

### C1 5: Information structure and register

- c1-5-1: Theme, focus and deliberate foregrounding — Organise information around a reader’s known context and the intended new or contrasting point.
- c1-5-2: Scope-sensitive restriction and emphasis — Control what only, also, even and negation apply to in complex statements.
- c1-5-3: Interactional particles and tone — Interpret conversational stance and choose particles appropriate to a real interaction.
- c1-5-4: Functional-verb constructions — Use conventional noun–verb combinations and distinguish action, onset and state.
- c1-5-5: Spoken and written standard — Recognise conversational structures while producing clear standard prose for formal contexts.
- c1-5-6: Economy, parallelism and clear revision — Remove grammatical ambiguity and unnecessary density while retaining meaning and appropriate emphasis.

### C1 6: Orthography and cumulative production

- c1-6-1: Punctuation as structure and meaning — Use commas, semicolons and quotation boundaries to express clause structure and intended attachment.
- c1-6-2: Capitalisation, compounds and common distinctions — Apply structural spelling decisions in careful writing rather than relying on sound alone.
- c1-6-3: Formal requests and negotiated conditions — Combine politeness, obligation, conditions and clear reference in a professional message.
- c1-6-4: C1 argument: claims, concessions and conclusions — Build a coherent argument with controlled stance and grammatically explicit relationships.
- c1-6-5: C1 report: sources, time and agency — Write a structured report that keeps chronology, reporting and responsibility clear.
- c1-6-6: C1 cumulative diagnosis and revision — Combine advanced grammar choices and develop a repeatable self-correction process.
